## 5. Don't Dump All 1000 USDT Into Futures

Having 1000 USDT ≠ transferring 1000 USDT to Futures.

Beginner fund layering:
- Spot funds: long-term / learning
- Futures risk capital: only money you can afford to lose completely
- Single-trade max loss: 1%–2% of total capital

Example: 1000 USDT total

- Single-trade risk: 10–20 USDT
- Leverage: 2x–5x

## 7. Long / Short — How to Choose?

Long: Expect price to rise
Short: Expect price to fall

Answer 4 questions BEFORE entry:
1. Why am I entering?
2. Where does this thesis become wrong?
3. Where is my Stop Loss?
4. Maximum USDT I'm willing to lose on this trade?

Guessing direction without levels = gambling.

## 8. Isolated vs Cross (Beginners → Isolated)

Isolated:
- Position uses its own independent margin
- Loses only that margin, then liquidates
- Easier to calculate "max loss per trade"

Cross:
- Entire Futures wallet balance backs the position
- One bad trade can drain the whole Futures wallet

## 9. Leverage Is NOT a "Win Rate Button"

2x / 3x / 5x / 10x / 20x / 125x

100 USDT margin:
- 2x → 200 USDT notional
- 10x → 1000 USDT notional
- 100x → 10,000 USDT notional

Price moves 1%:
- 2x PnL ≈ 2%
- 10x PnL ≈ 10%
- 100x PnL ≈ 100% (instant liquidation territory)

High leverage = volatility amplifier, NOT a profit hack.

## 10. Position Size Is the Real Risk Core

Don't just ask "how many x?"
Ask: "If Stop Loss hits, how many USDT leave my account?"

Risk formula (simplified):
Single-trade risk (USDT) = Position Size × |Entry Price − SL Price|
Or: Risk % ≈ Stop Loss Distance % × Leverage

Three things control risk:
1. Stop Loss distance
2. Position size
3. Leverage

## 12. What Is Mark Price?

Mark Price = Fair price used for unrealized PnL and liquidation. Derived from spot index + funding basis. Does NOT always equal Last Price.

Why it matters: Prevents a single wick from liquidating a fundamentally sound position.

Watch Last for entries. Watch Mark for risk.

---

## 13. What Is Liquidation Price?

When margin is insufficient to maintain a position, the system force-closes at Mark Price.

Beginner tragedy:
"I'll just hold, it'll come back" → hits liquidation → account gone.

Correct approach: Set Stop Loss BEFORE opening. Let the system execute. Don't rely on willpower.

---

## 14. Set Stop Loss FIRST — Not After Losing

Bad sequence:
Open → Lose → Hesitate → Hold → Liquidated

Good sequence:
Define invalidation level → Set SL → Calculate max loss → Size position → Open

SL is not cowardice. It's the rope that keeps you alive.

## 15. Take Profit Needs a Plan Too

When price moves in your favor, don't just scream "moon."

Exit methods:
- Partial Close (scale out)
- Tiered TP
- Full close
- Trailing Stop (move SL to breakeven / profit)

No exit plan = the classic "profitable trade turns into a loss" story.

## 16. Market Order vs Limit Order

Market: Executes at current available price. Fast. But slippage can be brutal in volatile conditions.
Limit: You set the price. Better control. But may not fill.

In high volatility, Market orders often fill at significantly worse prices than displayed.

## 19. Unrealized PnL ≠ Realized Money

Rule: Unrealized profit can be protected by moving SL, but never treat "on-screen" as "bank balance."

## 21. What Is Funding Fee?

Perpetual contracts typically settle Funding Fee every 8 hours (check platform schedule). Longs pay Shorts when Funding Rate is positive; Shorts pay Longs when negative.

If you hold through Funding windows, this cost affects net PnL. Must be calculated for high-frequency or swing positions.

## 23. Don't Martingale (Adding to Losers)

Loss → Add → Loss → Add more = exponential position growth, exponential risk.

Adding to winners is a strategy. Adding to losers "to average down" is the most expensive sentence in trading.

## 25. Margin Ratio — The Silent Killer

Margin Ratio = (Maintenance Margin / Margin Balance) × 100

As this ratio rises toward 100%, you're closer to liquidation. Many traders only watch Liq Price but ignore Margin Ratio trend. If it's climbing while price is moving against you, you're on borrowed time.

## 26. ADL (Auto-Deleveraging) — The Rare but Real Risk

In extreme volatile events, if the insurance fund is insufficient, Binance may ADL profitable traders' positions to cover bankrupt accounts. You can check your ADL rank/queue in the Futures interface. Lower rank = safer.
