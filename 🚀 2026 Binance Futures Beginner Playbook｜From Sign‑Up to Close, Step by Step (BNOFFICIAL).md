## 💰 Spot vs Futures
- Spot: buy BTC/ETH/BNB/SOL with USDT, own the asset, no leverage liquidation.
- Futures: take price direction with Long/Short; involves margin, leverage, liquidation, funding. P&L is more sensitive to small price moves when leverage is high.

## 🧮 Position sizing first
Example 1000 USDT total: keep emergency/spending money out, move only “full‑loss acceptable” amount to futures; beginners can start 50–200 USDT for process practice. Decide maximum loss first, then reverse‑engineer notional size and required margin.

## 🎯 Long / Short
- Long: profit if price rises.
- Short: profit if price falls.
Before entry write: thesis, invalidation price, maximum acceptable loss.

## 🔐 Isolated margin
Each position uses separate margin; losses are generally limited to that allocation and are easier to isolate. Good for beginners.

## 🔁 Cross margin
Available futures balance can support positions jointly; adverse moves may affect a wider pool of futures funds and increase account‑level liquidation risk. Use after experience.

## 🎚 Leverage
Common 2x/3x/5x/10x/20x and higher on some pairs. Leverage magnifies both profit and loss. Beginners often start 1x–5x; choose leverage after stop‑loss distance and affordable loss, not before.

## 📐 Position size math
Notional ≈ affordable loss ÷ |entry − stop| (adjusted for contract specs). Required initial margin ≈ notional ÷ leverage. This matters more than “which multiple”.

## 💲 Entry / Mark / Liquidation
- Entry Price: average fill price.
- Mark Price: reference price for risk/liquidation estimates; may differ from Last Price.[8](@ref)
- Liquidation Price: estimate only; affected by margin mode, maintenance margin tier, fees, collateral and equity.[8](@ref) Do not treat it as a stop.

## 🛑 Stop Loss
Flow: define invalidation → set SL → calculate max loss → set size → open. Use Reduce‑Only on SL/TP orders to avoid accidentally opening an opposite position in volatile spikes.[5](@ref)

## 🏁 Take Profit
Set first/second targets, partial close and full close rules before entry. Profit plan prevents emotional exits.

## ⚡ Market vs 📝 Limit
Market = fast, possible slippage. Limit = price control, possible no fill. Time‑in‑force options include GTC/IOC/FOK; Post‑Only helps maker routing.[5](@ref)

## 🧪 First demo‑style trade
Small USDT → Futures → BTCUSDT → Isolated → 2x–5x → small size → SL → TP → Market/Limit Long/Short → after fill, watch Entry/Mark/Liq/PnL. Goal is process mastery, not big profit.

## 📋 Monitor after open
Entry, Mark, Position Size, Margin, Leverage, Unrealized PnL, Liquidation Price, SL, TP, Funding Rate and next funding time.

## 💹 Unrealized / Realized PnL
Unrealized = open positions, changes with price. Realized = after close. Floating profit is not locked profit.

## 🔚 Close positions
Market Close, Limit Close, Partial Close, Full Close. Learn the close buttons in a small position first.

## 💸 Funding Fee
Perpetual swaps periodically exchange payment between longs and shorts, commonly every 8 hours; positive rate usually long pays short, negative rate reverse.[11](@ref) Funding is not the same as open/close commission.

## 🧾 Cost list
Open commission, close commission, funding, spread, slippage, and opportunity cost of locked margin. High frequency compounds all of these.

## 🚫 No revenge averaging
Adding after every loss without a pre‑set plan rapidly increases total risk. If scaling in, define total budget, max position, signals and drawdown limit.

## 🎢 Avoid chasing
Chasing pumps/shorts during crashes increases exposure to pullbacks, false breakouts and stop hunts. Trade a plan, not emotions.

## ❌ Common beginner mistakes
Wrong side, too much leverage, too large size, no SL, ignoring Mark/Liq, all funds in futures, averaging losers, no TP, ignoring funding, overtrading.
