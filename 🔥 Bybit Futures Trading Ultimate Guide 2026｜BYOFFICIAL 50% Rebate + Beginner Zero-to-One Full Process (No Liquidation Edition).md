## 2. How Are Bybit Futures Different From Spot?

Many beginners start with spot trading, then learn futures.

But futures and spot have completely different risk structures.

| Comparison Item | Spot Trading | Futures Trading |
|-----------------|--------------|-----------------|
| Trading Logic | Buy and hold assets | Trade price direction |
| Can Short | Not convenient | Can short |
| Use Leverage | Usually no | Can use leverage |
| Has Liquidation | No futures liquidation | May be liquidated |
| Risk Speed | Relatively slow | Can be very fast |
| Beginner Difficulty | Lower | Higher |
| Main Focus | Buy price & holding period | Leverage, position, stop-loss, liquidation |

When spot prices drop, it's usually just paper loss.

With futures and leverage, even a short-term reverse fluctuation can quickly approach the liquidation price.

So beginners should NOT treat futures like spot trading.

- **Spot buy BTC**: BTC drops 10%, you lose 100 USDT, BTC is still in your account
- **10x leverage long BTC**: BTC drops 10%, you lose 1000 USDT (entire capital), instantly liquidated to zero

## 9. How to Choose Between Market and Limit Orders?

### Market Order

A market order executes at the current market price.

Pros:

- Fast execution
- Good for quick entry/exit
- Less likely to miss moves

Cons:

- Possible slippage
- Fill price may be unfavorable during volatility
- Higher cost for large orders

### Limit Order

A limit order lets you set your desired price and wait for the market to reach it.

Pros:

- More price control
- Good for pre-planning
- Easier to control entry point

Cons:

- May not fill
- Requires waiting
- Market may move away

Beginners should learn limit orders first, then cautiously use market orders.

Don't emotionally chase orders just because the market is moving fast.

## 10. How to Choose Isolated vs Cross Margin?

Bybit futures has two common margin modes: isolated and cross.

### Isolated Margin

Each position uses independent margin.

If that position loses, it mainly affects that position itself.

For beginners, isolated margin is easier to understand and more convenient for controlling single-trade risk.

### Cross Margin

Cross margin uses all available funds in the futures account to jointly bear risk.

If the market moves sharply against you, it may affect the entire futures account balance.

| Mode | Feature | Beginner Advice |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| Isolated | Single-trade risk clearer | More suitable for beginners |
| Cross | Uses account funds to support positions | Beginners use with caution |

If you don't fully understand futures risks yet, start with isolated margin.

Quick reference:

| Leverage | Risk Feeling |

Beginners are better off with:

- Small capital
- Low leverage
- Isolated margin
- Clear stop-loss
- No chasing
- No over-positioning
- No holding losing trades

Futures trading is not about who uses higher leverage — it's about who has more stable risk control.

## 12. Always Check Liquidation Price

Liquidation price is a very important number in futures trading.

When market price approaches liquidation price, it means position risk is already very high.

Once liquidation is triggered, the system may auto-close your position.

Beginners should develop the habit of:

- Checking liquidation price immediately after opening
- Not letting price get too close to liquidation
- Not using high leverage to stubbornly hold against the market
- Not adding to losing positions
- Setting stop-loss before liquidation happens

Common liquidation path:

High leverage → Heavy position → No stop-loss → Adding to losers → Liquidation.

Avoiding this path matters more than guessing the market direction correctly.

## 13. How to Set Take-Profit and Stop-Loss?

### Take-Profit

Take-profit means actively locking in profits when price reaches your target.

For example, if you're long on BTC and price reaches your planned level, you can close partially or fully.

Take-profit functions:

- Lock in gains
- Avoid giving back profits
- Improve trading discipline

### Stop-Loss

Stop-loss means actively limiting losses when the market doesn't match your expectation.

For example, if you're long on BTC but price drops below your risk level, you can exit with stop-loss.

Stop-loss functions:

- Control single-trade loss
- Avoid approaching liquidation
- Protect principal
- Avoid emotional holding of losers

In futures trading, stop-loss is more important than prediction.

You can't be right about direction every time, but you CAN control the cost of each mistake.
